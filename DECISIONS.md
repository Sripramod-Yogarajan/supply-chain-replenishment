unchanged). The manual's rules, research objectives, datasets and provenance requirements are
not altered by anything below.

| D-14 | 2026-11-06 | **Clarifications.** (a) `reward_scale: auto` = sum of the stores' `avg_d` (116.2), not all nodes (which would count the same demand three times). | D-19 | 2026-11-15 | **Joint grid search for baseline tuning, with refinement.** The manual's coordinate search (store, then warehouse, then store) was run first. A 2-D check showed it stopped 3% above the joint optimum of the moving-average baseline (45,044 vs 43,832 on training days), because the store and warehouse parameters interact: stronger store targets pay off only with a leaner warehouse. Tuning is now joint: every pair of the coarse grid, then every pair on a 0.05 grid within one coarse step of the best pair, extending any edge the best pair sits on. Objective unchanged (mean total cost = holding + stockout over 5 training seeds). Tuning on the full cost (adding order changes) was also tried and gives nearly the same values (base-stock 1.30/1.20 vs 1.35/1.10; moving average 2.05/0.40 vs 2.10/0.40), so the manual's objective is kept (`baselines.tuning_objective`). | Manual 10.3 tuning procedure replaced by a stronger search; objective, grids and data split unchanged. Weakly tuned baselines would flatter the agent. |
| D-20 | 2026-11-15 | **Evaluation set-up.** Evaluation seeds are 1 to 20; tuning seeds are 101 to 105 (training days only, so no overlap of days with the test). Scenarios live in `config.yaml` (`scenarios`): shock window days 1750 to 1759 (the manual's example), transit delay W1->St1 +3 days, demand spike St1 x1.5, price discount St1 `d` 0.2 with `u` pending (A14), so that scenario is skipped until `u` is chosen. The control run is scored over the same window, so a shock's effect is read as scenario minus control under the same seeds. The warm-up rule R9 is reported as a third, naive reference row. Base-stock and moving average are also compared per seed (`results/paired.csv`). | Manual 10.3 says sizes come "from the config"; they were not defined there. Reference window, naive row and paired comparison are additions to reporting only. |
| D-21 | 2026-11-15 | **Sensitivity: re-tune per cost ratio.** For stockout-to-holding ratios 10 and 40 both baselines are re-tuned on training days before evaluation (the cost-optimal target depends on the ratio); the starting-stock variants (x0.5, x1.5) keep the tuned values. | Manual 10.3 lists the variants but not whether to re-tune; re-tuning gives each baseline its best chance in every variant. |
| D-22 | 2026-11-15 | **Generated results are not committed.** `results/` (tuned parameters, metrics, summaries) and `runs/` are in `.gitignore`; they are rebuilt by the commands in the README, and the chosen values and headline numbers are recorded in `reports/phase_3.md`. | Follows D-5. |
| D-23 | 2026-11-16 | **S3 uplift `u = 0.2`, estimated from M5** (owner chose the data-based option over a textbook value). Method and caveats in A21; code `supply_chain_rl.data.uplift`. A second scenario `price_discount_high` (`u = 0.5`) is evaluated as well, because the estimate is uncertain and probably on the low side for a short promotion. For the baselines, which never look at price, a discount with uplift `u` is identical to a demand spike with `k = 1 + u` (verified: `price_discount_high` reproduces `demand_spike` exactly); the difference will matter for the agent, which sees the price. | Fills the manual's open parameter (7.3); completes checkpoint P3 item 3. |

| D-25 | 2026-11-16 | **Gate G2 criteria fixed.** On validation days (1507 to 1700 decision days), control scenario, evaluation seeds 1 to 20, the tuned baselines give: base-stock total cost 5,157 ± 328, fill 90.0%, chain bullwhip 6.40; moving average 5,394 ± 355, fill 92.6%, chain bullwhip 19.55; warm-up rule 10,861, fill 99.5%, bullwhip 0.99 (`reports/reproduction.md` Section 4).
